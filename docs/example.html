Worked example

Merriwa Park Shopping Centre

The dataset examples/merriwa-park-dataset.json is a fictional mall precinct: one site, three car parks, and seven vehicle entrances and exits. Profile static-rules, schema version 0.2.0, dataset version 2026-11-23.1.

Fictional. The file’s own note says Merriwa Park Shopping Centre, its roads, identifiers, prices, coordinates and organisations are invented. It is not a real place, and the organisations named in it have not endorsed OPMS. Jurisdiction examples in the draft are illustrations, not legal advice.

Open the explorer to draw this dataset from the coordinates in the file, or switch to the smaller single-level example examples/simple-carpark-dataset.json.

The precinct

Site mpsc is a shopping_centre in timezone Australia/Melbourne, locality class metro_suburban, governed in the example by jurisdiction jur.au-vic. ticket_scope is facility, so one ticket does not cover every complex by default. The three facilities share one local frame.

The drawing below is a schematic. It places the north multi-deck toward Banksia Street and Merriwa Road, the basement beneath, and the east lot toward Wattle Avenue, because those are the names in the file. It is not a surveyed plan, and the example does not give a site outline.

Where people are dropped off

Destinations in the file are the cinema on mall level 2, the food court on mall level 1, the supermarket on mall ground, and the east entrance on mall ground. Three handover zones are recorded.

  • mpsc.hz.north is on north-deck ground, kind both drop-off and pick-up, and is marked as an ISO 23374 handover. Capacity 6 vehicles, maximum dwell 180 seconds. The accessibility record includes a 2.7 m kerbside transfer space, a kerb ramp, a step-free route, shelter, seating, an assistance point, tactile marking, good lighting, and a 300 second wait allowance.
  • mpsc.hz.east is on the east lot, also kind both, and is not an ISO 23374 handover. Capacity 4, dwell 120 seconds. It has a kerb ramp and a step-free flag, a 1.2 m transfer space, partial lighting, no shelter, no seating and no assistance point.
  • mpsc.hz.rideshare is on the fictional Wattle Avenue kerb segment cs.wattle-ave.n.0-40. Capacity 3, dwell 120 seconds. It is not an ISO 23374 handover.

Destination access from the north handover zone to the cinema records a 150 second, 160 metre walk, step-free and covered. The linked accessible path mpsc.path.hz-north-cinema records the same 160 metre distance, a 260 second walk time, a 4% maximum gradient, a 1.5 m minimum width, tactile indicators, full shelter, two rest points and three seating points. The specification’s lifecycle uses the 150 second figure. Both numbers are in the example. They are illustrative.

The supermarket is 90 seconds and 80 metres from the east handover zone, and 60 seconds and 45 metres from Zone A on B1. That second route has an accessible path of 45 metres through lift mpsc.lift.p2-a, with one vehicle crossing called out as a hazard.

How the specification uses this site

Section 11 of the specification is an informative sequence, not a manufacturer’s design. A passenger app asks the vehicle to go to the cinema. The vehicle fetches the static-rules dataset and the change feed, checks the quality tier, and chooses the north handover zone and entry mpsc.ap1. The passenger walks from that handover zone. The car parks using anchors and zone order. The sequence says the map can be prefetched and that no live data is required. On summon, the car refreshes the change feed, picks an exit, and may contribute redacted, pseudonymous observations of features that changed.

Publication terms on the dataset allow non-automated-vehicle use, bulk download, JSON and GeoJSON, and name an example accessibility contact. The public URL is on opms.example.org, which the repository treats as a placeholder.
