Specification

What v0.3 defines

This page is an overview of SPEC.md. The JSON Schemas are normative. The text of the specification is CC BY 4.0. The schemas and tools are Apache-2.0. Schema $id URLs use opms.example.org until a permanent home exists.

Draft for discussion, version 0.3.0, dated 2026-11-23. Not published as a standard. No company or standards body has endorsed it. Breaking changes are expected before 1.0.

What v0.3 adds

v0.3 gives physical features coordinates in the facility frame and a level id, including entrances, gates, lifts, stair cores, drop-off zones, destinations, zones and the site boundary. Paths and drive edges can carry a centreline, required when the facility tier is 2 or 3 or the feature is owner-verified. Portals and destinations reference level_id. Each feature can store verification records, and review.schema.json is a standalone sign-off. The review tool is not part of this draft. See the specification section on located geometry.

Purpose and non-goals

OPMS describes the static structure of parking places and the records that let many parties build and maintain that structure by crowdsourcing. Places include street kerbs, surface lots, multi-storey and underground garages, mechanical parking, private garages, and large sites such as shopping centres, airports and hospitals.

It is a glue layer. It does not replace HD map formats or facility-data standards. It links to them by identifier.

Out of scope in v0.3: live space availability, except as an optional extension; wire protocols between a vehicle and a facility; vehicle control; and payment processing.

Profiles

A dataset declares one cumulative profile. schema/dataset.schema.json enforces it. A conforming consumer must work with static-core alone. Live occupancy is never required.

Profiles from section 3 of the specification. Each row adds to the rows above it.
Profile What it adds Notes
static-core Frames, facilities, levels, zones, bays, the drive graph, access points, gates, links, anchors and pillars. Default. No rules, destinations or live data. Enough for a car to localise, route and search.
static-rules Destinations, portals, handover zones, kerb segments, rules and rates. Site semantics and regulations.
dynamic An optional live feed. Not required by anything else.
services Reservations, sessions and missions. Reserved. Not schematised in v0.3.

Conventions

  • Each facility has a local Cartesian frame in metres, x east, y north, z up, anchored to WGS84. Indoor geometry is local. Road links and site outlines are WGS84. Level ordinal 0 is ground. Negative ordinals are below ground.
  • Units are SI, with km/h for signed speed limits. Money is a decimal string plus an ISO 4217 currency.
  • Times are RFC 3339 with an offset. Time windows are local to the site’s IANA timezone. 24:00 is allowed as an end.
  • Identifiers are lower-case dotted strings, stable, and never reused. External identifiers live in external_refs.
  • Unknown means not allowed. A missing rule is not permission. A missing clearance blocks oversize vehicles. Unverified features are not used for driverless operation.
  • Objects may carry extension properties prefixed x_. Other unknown properties are rejected.

Schema files

One file per object, plus the dataset, contribution-bundle and registry roots, and shared definitions in common.schema.json. All are JSON Schema draft 2020-12. Field tables are generated in the specification from these files.

Crowdsourcing

Several vehicle makers, survey firms, operators and authorities may contribute static layout only. Contributions stay separate from the consolidated map. The pipeline in the specification is:

  1. A repository or authority publishes a campaign for a facility that is missing, stale, disputed or below its target tier.
  2. A contributor submits a bundle: a contribution, feature observations and alignment records, and attests what was redacted. In a licensed-unit jurisdiction the bundle goes to the licensed unit first.
  3. The repository aligns observations, updates evidence, resolves conflicts, applies quality-tier rules and publishes a change set.
  4. Consumers read the consolidated dataset and the change feed. They never need the raw contributions.

Quality tiers run from 0 (indicative, display and planning) to 3 (driverless). The numeric accuracy targets are proposals for discussion, not requirements of any regulator. Driverless use in the draft expects an audit, a certification on file, and operator consent that covers driverless_use.

Governance and privacy

A jurisdiction names the repository, who may submit, residency (unrestricted, in_country_only, or cross_border_with_approval) and the approval regime. Operator consent is a separate approval on the facility. Redaction policy is what keeps plates, identities and trajectories out. The registry example shows an Australian-style direct jurisdiction and a China-style licensed-unit jurisdiction. Both are illustrative, not legal advice.

Passenger needs are stored and processed on the device only. Consent is explicit, scoped and revocable. Neither passenger needs nor handover requirements contain a diagnosis, a condition name or an identity, and neither appears in a map dataset.

Conformance

  • A static-core producer emits a dataset that validates against dataset.schema.json with profile: static-core.
  • A static-core consumer accepts any valid static-core dataset and ignores x_ properties.
  • A repository also publishes change sets with tombstones, enforces its jurisdiction’s routing and redaction policy, and keeps raw contributions out of published datasets.
  • tools/validate.py checks schema validity, example validity and reference integrity.

Open questions in the draft

  1. Who hosts and signs site datasets: operator, council, neutral registry or consortium.
  2. A small common facility API for mission, gate and payment calls beyond ISO 23374.
  3. Accuracy targets per tier, and how audits are done.
  4. Mapping to ISO/TS 22726-1:2025, NDS.Live and OpenDRIVE 1.9.
  5. Public holidays, event days, permit areas, and signs that conflict on the ground.
  6. Liability when map data is wrong, and how a car proves it parked legally.
  7. Schemas for the services layer.
  8. Whether SENSORIS should gain a parking-facility campaign type.
  9. Interoperability with MAZE, and which APDS v5 classes to reference.
  10. How OPMS maps to the Chinese draft standard plan 20243535-T-466.
  11. Which accessibility fields people with lived experience consider essential, missing or harmful.
  12. How to verify accessibility claims at scale without exposing people.
